Project Overview

Goal: Predict which employees are at risk of leaving so HR can intervene before attrition happens. The dataset comes from IBM's publicly released HR Analytics sample — 1,470 employees, 35 original features, 16.1% attrition rate.

Starting with all 44 candidate predictors (after encoding), the model used backward elimination — dropping the least significant variable one at a time — until only factors with p < 0.01 remained. The final model uses 20 predictors.

Performance was evaluated on a held-out 20% test set. At a 30% probability cutoff, the model catches 70% of actual leavers while keeping false alarms manageable — a practical threshold for HR screening.

Why logistic regression? It produces a probability score (0–1) for each employee, not just a yes/no label. That means HR can rank employees by risk level, set their own threshold based on capacity to intervene, and explain the decision using actual coefficients — something black-box models can't do.

The analysis was also reproduced in Excel to make results accessible to non-technical stakeholders and verify that the Python output held up under a different implementation.

Key Finding
AUC = 0.85  ·  70% recall

At a 30% probability cutoff, the model correctly identifies 70% of employees who actually leave — while the baseline rate in the data is only 16.1%. That's a 4x improvement in targeting efficiency compared to random screening.

OverTime is the single strongest predictor: employees who work overtime are significantly more likely to leave, all else equal. Satisfaction variables (job, environment, relationship) consistently act as buffers. The Excel reproduction matched the Python output on all major coefficients, validating the approach across both implementations.

What I Did
  • Cleaned and preprocessed the raw Kaggle dataset — dropped constant columns, applied one-hot encoding to categoricals, and performed a stratified 80/20 train/test split
  • Built the backward elimination loop in Python: starting from 44 predictors, iteratively dropping the highest p-value variable each round until all remaining factors were significant at p < 0.01
  • Evaluated model performance using AUC, confusion matrix, and precision/recall at multiple probability cutoffs to find a practical threshold for HR use
  • Reproduced the full analysis in Excel — logistic regression via Solver, coefficient table, and probability scoring — to make results accessible to non-technical stakeholders
